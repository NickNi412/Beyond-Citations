# Audit card: creatine and kidney safety

This is the final output of the pipeline for one AI-written report, after a researcher carried out the checks by hand. The program produced the structure, the order of checks and the guarantees; a person read the cited papers and filled in the results.

**Claim (from the AI report).** Long-term creatine use at standard doses is safe for kidney function in healthy adults.

**Target.** The claim should survive any one wrong study.

**Status: NOT CERTIFIED.**

## Conditions, stated per study

For the claim to hold, at least one study must satisfy all three:

- **A. Measurement.** Kidney function measured in a way creatine does not distort (measured GFR or cystatin C, not creatinine-based estimates).
- **B. Population.** Healthy adults.
- **C. Duration.** Use over years.

## Checks, in the order the plan gives

Measurement is checked first because it is the shared condition that most routes depend on.

| Order | Check | Result | Studies removed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A. Measurement | Kreider 2003, Mayhew 2002 and Poortmans 1999 use creatinine-based markers: fail | 3 of 5 |
| 2 | C. Duration | Neves 2011 and Lugaresi 2013 use measured GFR but last 12 weeks: fail | the remaining 2 |

Seven checks settled the claim; checking every condition on every study would have taken fifteen.

## What the evidence does support

- Creatinine-based kidney markers stay normal for years in healthy athletes.
- Measured kidney function does not fall over 12 weeks of supplementation.

## Next check

## What this card does not say

It does not show that creatine harms kidneys. It says that the evidence checked so far does not establish the claim as stated.
